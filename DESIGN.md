# Design

## RaceSide logo system

The approved «Траектория» identity replaces the former RS monogram. Its compact S-shaped symbol consists of two counterposed solid ribbons with an open racing line between them. The symbol, Geist Sans 800 outlined RaceSide wordmark and Geist Sans 500 descriptor form one fixed logo. Use the shared `RaceMateLogo`, `RaceMateMark` and `RaceSideLogoSvg` components from `src/components/racemate/racemate-logo.tsx`; their vector paths live in `raceside-logo-artwork.ts`. Regenerate artwork and exports with `scripts/generate-raceside-brand.mjs`. Never rebuild the name with live text beside a separately scaled mark. Public vector variants and the font license live in `public/brand/raceside/`.

Use the signature with «Гоночный центр» in the desktop sidebar, mobile header, footer and expanded admin header. Full-size comparison, telemetry and prediction feed cards use the same signature. The navigation loading panel, compact LIVE/Replay header, compact Open Graph prediction cards and Grand Prix recap cards use the horizontal logo without the descriptor to fit their short rows. The collapsed admin sidebar uses the standalone symbol. Square avatars, favicon and Apple Touch Icon use the symbol with safe optical margins. The vertical lockup includes the descriptor and remains available for square brand compositions.

RaceSide Plus adds a fixed outlined «+» to the right of the wordmark; the standalone Plus symbol adds the same cross beside the top of the trajectory mark. Use `RaceSideLogoSvg plus` in the Plus invitation, and `RaceMateMark plus` in the account dock and active-subscription badge. Above the Plus page's main headline, keep the original compact text label «RaceSide Plus». The cross is part of the vector artwork, never separately positioned text. The base navigation and footer retain the standard identity. Public Plus screenshot branding is rebuilt locally with `scripts/refresh-raceside-brand-previews.mjs`; everything outside the logo regions must remain pixel-identical.

The Telegram support identity reuses the approved trajectory symbol and outlined RaceSide wordmark, adds a small dialogue badge and uses «Поддержка» as its service descriptor. Its transparent dark/light and monochrome SVGs, standalone symbols and square avatars are generated by `scripts/generate-raceside-support-brand.mjs` into `public/brand/telegram/` and `output/telegram-bot-kit-20261006/`. This service badge belongs to support; the Plus cross remains reserved for RaceSide Plus. The two Telegram welcome banners are raster campaign assets; reuse exact vector logo files for other placements.

Theme-aware logo colors use `--racemate-logo-mark`, `--racemate-logo-wordmark` and `--racemate-logo-descriptor`: the mark stays Ferrari red, while the name and descriptor follow the light/dark background. Server-generated share images use an explicit palette. Scale each lockup proportionally, preserve clear space and keep shadows, gradients and effects out of the logo itself. Public screenshots on the Plus page must carry the same approved artwork.

## Completed Grand Prix report

The existing `raceReport` popup contains one scrollable report with a fixed heading and four section links: «Обзор», «Позиции и отрывы», «Темп и стратегия», «Итоги». Desktop uses a wide Dialog; mobile uses the full viewport and a compact section selector. Keep the existing URL, close behavior and sharing action. Preserve the current AI/news text under «Что обсуждали вокруг этапа». Do not show key-event timelines, «Что решило гонку», generated causal explanations or strategic verdicts.

The overview starts with a horizontal podium and compact race statistics. Large charts use solid team-color traces, readable axes and explicit gaps for missing data. Tyre history uses one chronological scale with compound letters; pit-stop time and full pit-lane duration remain separate. Results include each driver’s best lap, points, start-to-finish changes and teammate comparisons. Chart tooltips show positions or time gaps in seconds. Race-pace axes and tooltips use minutes and seconds (m:ss), with milliseconds in tooltips. Raw-data disclosures are omitted. The report footer shows only its update time. Championship comparisons use the exact before/after round snapshots and remain unavailable when either snapshot is incomplete. Report graphs use stored server-side data from the main Race session; practice, sprint and qualifying never supply race-report charts. Compact report timing and race pace are prepared independently of the map-heavy Replay archive. Ready Race Replay timing remains a fallback, with bounded concurrent reads. Empty or partial analytics are not retained in the long-lived report cache; an open, visible report checks for late timing every 30 seconds and selects available pilots unless the user has already made a choice. Complete expired analytics stay available during a background cache refresh. The full interactive Telemetry Hub retains its existing Plus access. No missing timing, tyre, flag or standings information is invented. Existing light/dark tokens, Geist fonts, visible focus, reduced motion and mobile readability apply.

## Shared Grand Prix recap

The completed-race share PNG uses the approved 1080 × 1350 editorial card: horizontal RaceSide logo, a restrained single-line race title, compact P1–P3 row, a large all-driver position chart, four aligned statistics, a two-column top ten and the exact after-round championship top three. The statistics order is «Лучший круг», «Больше всего позиций», «На финише», «Команда этапа»; pilot/team names and retirement counts sit on their own line below the value. Team-of-the-weekend points include the sprint when present. Omit «Итоги за минуту», the section headings «Позиции по кругам» and «Очковая десятка», news, track/car illustrations and the former strategy/pit/flag sections from this card. Use the freed heading space for the position chart. The popup retains its news text. Use saved report timing, break lines at missing laps and stop at the last observation; never create positions from final classification. Keep all participants in the compact legend, solid team traces and slight brightness variation between teammates. Missing timing or championship snapshots use a short empty state. The existing preview, PNG download and native sharing remain the entry points.

The recap’s «На финише» statistic follows the final result status, not the number of completed laps. `Lapped` and `+N Lap(s)` count as finished, including drivers several laps behind. Retired drivers remain excluded even if they completed nearly the whole distance. Apply the same status check to retirement markers and position-gain highlights.

## Telemetry workspace

The shared Grand Prix/session strip follows the selected third variant: a compact 260px Grand Prix menu beside separate 44px session buttons, with a subtle red selected state. Buttons come from the selected meeting's actual session list, including sprint weekends, and wrap without horizontal scrolling. Session-comparison and race-pace setup keep only the Grand Prix menu in this row. Settings dialogs reuse the same controls with a compact season column. Below 900px the session buttons occupy their own row; on mobile the Grand Prix menu fills the available width and shares its row with the season menu inside settings.

The setup-only introduction matches the News header's 160px desktop height at 1024px and up, with the same 184px minimum below that breakpoint. The open comparison header keeps its content-based height. Setup modes use one compact segmented control with sentence-case Geist labels, small icons on desktop and a raised active segment; icons hide on mobile to preserve readable labels without scrolling. The comparison views reuse the same segmented surface, typography and raised active state. One primary row contains «Обзор», «Скорость», «Педали», «Мотор и передачи», «Трасса», «Сравнение участков», «Выходы и разгон», «Управление энергией» and «Разбор круга». The row fills the entire workspace width; tabs grow from their natural label widths to share remaining space. Below 1300px it switches to the existing compact select so every view stays available without clipped labels or horizontal scrolling. There is no second navigation row or «Весь круг» corner switch in the primary toolbar.

Telemetry traces use each driver's OpenF1 team color consistently in charts, labels, track segments and exported cards. Every trace is a solid line. Comparisons within one team or between two laps of one driver use the team color for the first trace and a neutral gray for the second; other comparisons keep both team colors. Missing team colors use a neutral fallback.

Race telemetry adds «Средний темп гонки» beside the regular lap choices. The resulting trace is one distance-normalized average of every comparable clean lap for that driver; pit-in laps, their following pit-out laps, deleted or incomplete laps, and laps affected by yellow, red, safety-car or virtual-safety-car periods are excluded. The summary states how many laps formed the average and omits tyre/compound context because the profile may span several stints.

While an existing comparison is edited, loading a new session catalog stays inside the settings dialog and keeps the current workspace visible. The full loading panel appears only for the submitted comparison. Whole-lap analysis labels braking distance as the combined path travelled with the brake pressed beyond the threshold, omits neutral gear 0, and uses bold weight for the stronger comparable value; braking positions remain unranked.

`/telemetry` uses a fixed viewport with the standard RaceSide navigation and no site footer, as requested on 12 September 2026. Charts fill the available height. Comparison controls open in a dialog after the first comparison; whole-lap analysis lives in its own tab. Mobile uses a view selector. Only long detail panels and dialogs scroll internally. Circuit outlines use the horizontal plane of the existing models with equal scaling on both axes.

Telemetry keeps the standard full RaceSide sidebar, mobile header, Geist typography, semantic colors, PageTitle and Button components. The navigation item follows Current Weekend. Setup is a compact session strip followed by two driver/lap selections with trace accents, using three user-facing scenarios: drivers/laps, sessions, and race pace. Best/manual laps share one selector; teammate comparison lives in the opponent selector. There are no duplicate preset actions. Saved comparisons remain compatible with the existing backend modes.

Selecting a corner on the regular track map shows its traversal time, entry/minimum/exit speed and local time advantage. Whole-lap sectors and gear usage stay in «Разбор круга», which always ignores the selected corner. All telemetry graphs mark corner apex positions above the distance axis; nearby labels use separate rows and respect the visible distance range.

«Сравнение участков» compares time gained in slow, medium and fast corners and on straights with four diverging bars on a common zero axis. Selecting a category highlights the same sections on the map. Detailed section tables are replaced by two compact rows for each driver's largest measurable gain across the lap and a speed comparison for the selected category. Corner speed is each driver's median of minimum speeds in that group; straights use the maximum measured speed. Values come from full-resolution samples before chart downsampling; unavailable values stay empty. Selecting a highlight opens its corner in «Выходы и разгон» or positions the cursor on the full-lap speed graph for a straight. The section heading also reads «Сравнение участков»; the subtitle is «Где выиграно время». Short desktop viewports use single-line summaries and tighter spacing; bars and the map caption stay visible without overlap. Shared approximate corner boundaries come from the circuit's apex metadata, braking and throttle; the speed classes are below 150, 150–230 and at least 230 km/h. Only continuous sections available for both drivers contribute to each bar, with partial coverage explicitly counted. Sector timing controls precision: unreliable local timing hides time gains while keeping speed evidence. This describes the chosen laps; it does not rank driver skill.

«Выходы и разгон» contains its own corner selector, a map and synchronized speed/throttle graphs with zero at the shared corner exit. A contrasting transverse strip on the map follows the graph cursor; there is no «Открыть на графике» button. Separate values show apex-to-exit, following-straight and combined time gains. The repeatability block is named «Повторяемость преимущества». It independently selects up to four distinct lap pairs for each interval in the same session, prioritizing proximity to the chosen laps. Flags, whole-lap validity, weather and tyre age are context, not eligibility gates. Show compound and known tyre ages beside each pair, wins out of checked pairs and the median difference, including neutral pairs. Missing sector checks use a muted estimate with «≈» and never count as a win. «Скорость», «Педали» and «Мотор и передачи» always show the complete lap and do not inherit or create corner zoom.

«Управление энергией» places the driver/lap selector to the right of its heading in the same row, followed by event filters, numbered map regions, a speed graph and compact throttle/brake values. Call sustained deceleration with pressed throttle and released brake «Суперклиппинг», following the product definition agreed on 7 October 2026. Put that definition before the graph. Keep coasting before braking and weakening acceleration as separate observations; any proposed relation to energy remains a hypothesis. Do not display battery charge, energy quantities or confirmed deployment from these channels. Averaged race traces cannot establish individual pedal events. Omit the «Эксперимент» badge, subtitle, separate pedal graphs and «Открыть на графике» action from this view, as requested on the same date. The profile has no «Оценка по телеметрии» badge. The expandable «Как читать этот разбор» block is removed from all three analysis views at the user's request; calculation rules remain in project documentation, while available coverage and unverified estimates remain visible in the data.

Telemetry uses the exact shared AppShell width limit (1440px including sidebar padding), without an additional inner width cap. Its title and driver comparison share one stage-style panel; setup has its own panel. The selected meeting supplies the localized Grand Prix title, with circuit and session below. View switches use the site's compact mono button styling; the Conditions tab is removed. The circuit panel is named «Карта трассы». Share images reuse RaceSideShareLogo, local driver portraits, Geist Sans/Mono, obsidian panels and the red bottom band from the existing driver-comparison share cards. Both fixed export orientations use the same identity and whole-lap data.

The chart toolbar contains one compact corner selector with previous/next arrows and a Whole Lap option. It is hidden in whole-lap Analysis. There is no duplicate row of corner buttons under the map and no zoom/pan mode selector. The distance slider subscribes to the same cursor store as charts and the track markers, and shows distance in metres. Map markers show each driver’s actual distance at the same elapsed lap time, with lap starts aligned at zero; driver labels and a compact time/distance gap clarify the leader even when the markers are close. The gap strip reserves a fixed height with three columns: leader, time gap and distance between the cars. Ties and missing data keep the same footprint, so scrubbing never resizes the map; distance from the start appears only on the slider.

Telemetry combines gear and RPM charts in «Мотор и передачи». DRS is absent from all Hub views and exports; raw cached provider records remain compatible. Grand Prix selectors omit the «Гран-при» prefix. Driver summaries are compact, and sector statistics sit at the bottom of the map panel. Telemetry sharing has exactly two fixed dashboard exports: horizontal 3200×1800 and vertical 2160×3840. The vector layout is rendered directly at twice its logical dimensions, including the nested graph SVGs. Both contain the comparison summary, weather, delta, speed, throttle, gear, track map and lap analysis; users choose only the orientation. Graph headers never show current or summary values. The lower graph axes combine a distance scale with numeric corner labels on compact separate rows; sector names sit on a separate row below the chart title, with neutral gray boundary lines inside the plot. Speed annotations reserve their own space below the sector labels. Speed graphs annotate at most four highest peaks and four deepest lows with both drivers’ speeds at the same distance. Swings below 35 km/h or 18% of the lap’s speed range are omitted; extrema of the same kind must be separated by at least 7% of the lap distance. The track map omits corner numbers and keeps sector boundary strokes and sector results. The vertical lap-analysis table uses larger labels and values for social-media readability. Graph axes and annotations use filled Geist Sans Medium outlines with tabular digits, generated from the existing local font. ## Design Direction

Reading this as: a motorsport product for engaged Russian-speaking F1 fans, with a premium sport-app language, leaning toward Stitch's dark `Apex Performance` cockpit system: obsidian surfaces, Ferrari red active states, neon-green live status, sharp editorial hierarchy, dense race-control modules, shadcn/ui primitives, and custom RaceSide tokens.

Scene sentence: a fan opens RaceSide on a phone or laptop in the hour before a race session, wants the key story, the next start time, and their prediction status immediately, and should feel the product is fast, current, and composed.

On `/weekend`, tyre allocation is a compact inline part of the hero: beside the event context on desktop and immediately below the track on mobile. It must consume existing hero space instead of increasing the fixed map/hero height.

Design dials:

- Design variance: 7/10.
- Motion intensity: 5/10.
- Visual density: 8/10 for app/admin surfaces, 6/10 for public editorial pages.

## Color System

Use semantic tokens in CSS based on the Stitch Apex Performance palette. Avoid purple gradients, beige/cream defaults, and one-note slate-only dark mode.

Strategy:

- Base: Obsidian Base `#0B0B0B`.
- Surface: Obsidian Elevated `#121212`, Obsidian Surface `#1E1E1E`, and hairline glass stroke `rgba(255,255,255,0.08)`.
- Public dark panels keep their existing grouping, borders, radius and spacing. Inside `AppShell`, use an opaque near-black `#0B0B0B` fill, a 6.5% white stroke and subtle depth: a 3.5% white inset top edge, a dark inset bottom edge, a short contact shadow and a tightly bounded ambient shadow. Apply the shared `--panel-depth-shadow` to glass/stitch panels and framed section/article/link card surfaces, retaining Tailwind focus rings in the shadow composition. Omit glass blur and gray top gradients; secondary and hover surfaces stay dark. Light theme and admin keep their existing treatment.
- Primary accent: Ferrari/F1 red `#E10600` for primary actions, active nav, current race/session emphasis.
- Secondary accent: Live neon `#39FF14` for live/available states only.
- Tertiary accent: timing amber for upcoming sessions, warnings, and lock windows.
- Team colors appear only as contextual accents, never as the global palette.

Contrast requirements:

- Body text on background and surfaces: at least 4.5:1.
- Muted text must remain readable and should not fall into decorative gray.
- Status colors must be paired with text, icons, or labels.

## Typography

Use `next/font`; do not link Google Fonts with raw `<link>` tags or CSS `@import`.

Recommended stack:

- UI and display: Geist Sans in production, tuned to match Stitch's Hanken Grotesk proportions.
- Timing, codes, session labels, and technical metadata: Geist Mono.

Stitch references may mention Hanken Grotesk, Inter, and JetBrains Mono. In this repo, use `geist` via `next/font` unless a local font file is added explicitly. Avoid serif as the default product font; RaceSide should feel sharp and modern, not magazine-vintage.

Type rules:

- H1/H2 use balanced wrapping.
- Body copy max width: 65-75ch.
- Dense UI headings stay compact; reserve hero scale for true public hero moments.
- Product UI uses a fixed rem scale rather than viewport-driven font scaling.
- Letter spacing stays at 0 for most UI; display tightening must not go below `-0.04em`.

## Layout

Public surfaces:

- Use a strong cockpit first viewport: current race context, next session, weather/session strip, season geography or track context, and one high-signal right rail.
- On `/`, the current track slot becomes the transparent interactive season globe specified in `13_home_season_globe.md`: the hero has no panel background, frame or shadow. The full sphere uses local relief, cold rim lighting, red routes and a limited set of circuit labels; the selected label shows a flag. No on-globe controls. Circuit names wrap without globe overlap. Circuit and race pages keep their verified track maps and 3D models. Mobile 3D opens directly in the shared fullscreen track viewer used by race pages, LIVE and Replay. Its 44px top controls group zoom and an expand/collapse action without a shared background or frame; no overhead toggle or separate close icon appears. Track facts sit at the bottom edge, with space for the fixed lower 2D/3D switch. Elevation hints explain approximate data without a visible approximation symbol; a one-finger drag pans, pinch zooms, two-finger rotation changes bearing, and a two-finger vertical swipe changes tilt. Gesture thresholds prevent accidental movement. Overview framing uses circuit geometry and reserves space for controls; portrait views orient elongated circuits along the phone height. Inline mobile previews preserve page scrolling. Desktop retains the orientation sphere, mouse and keyboard controls.
- News and race hub pages can use asymmetric layouts, but must collapse cleanly to a single-column mobile view.
- Team profile heroes show an unframed «О команде» definition list beside their statistics at desktop widths of 1280px and up, and below them on smaller screens. Keep the car area reserved when the list stacks. Show first entry, base, leadership, technical directors and chassis in that order, without a visible source link; first-entry years follow Formula1.com's team-profile convention, with provenance retained in the data catalog. The locally verified `src/data/team-information.ts` snapshot covers 2026 only and must never fall back into archived or future seasons. It does not introduce external requests or automatic refresh jobs.
- Avoid a generic landing page unless explicitly needed; the app experience should be visible immediately.

App surfaces:

- Favor dense but organized panels, tables, segmented controls, tabs, filters, and side/top navigation.
- Desktop uses the Stitch shell: fixed top navigation plus left command sidebar. Mobile collapses to a burger/drawer.
- Cards are for repeated content, modals, and framed tools. Do not nest cards.
- Fixed-format UI such as prediction picks, standings rows, and session cards must have stable dimensions to avoid layout jumps.
- The desktop next-session card retains a shared in-memory snapshot across page navigation. Only its first load uses a skeleton; background refreshes keep the last valid content visible, including after a network failure. Refresh every five minutes, every minute around an active session, at start/end boundaries and on returning to the tab; pause requests while hidden or below the desktop sidebar breakpoint. The countdown runs locally.

Historical season surfaces:

- Calendar, championship, teams, driver profiles, and team-lineage profiles keep the selected year only in their own `?season=YYYY`; championship mode keeps `table=constructors` as well. Main navigation opens the current season and never propagates an archive year.
- `SeasonSwitcher` sits under the surface title or profile metadata. On desktop it is collapsed by default, keeps the active year visible, and reveals the other published years horizontally on demand; it expands right by default and left in the team profile hero. On mobile it becomes a compact native select and never expands the page horizontally. The control uses a 44px interaction height, visible focus, keyboard-native behavior, 150-200ms feedback, and no transition under reduced motion.
- A profile with one available season shows a quiet static season label instead of a disabled control.
- Historical pages use the same Apex Performance hierarchy as the current season, but only show sporting information. Current-only news, social posts, odds, weather, AI race reports, and Race Replay do not appear.
- Historical calendar maps always use the same muted grayscale treatment as completed races in the current season; the active current-season race remains the only full-color state.
- Cars, logos, and maps must resolve from the selected season and remain part of its publication gate. Never substitute 2026 artwork for a historical asset.
- Historical driver portraits are optional. When a season-specific portrait is absent, show a composed number or initials placeholder with the team's seasonal color, without loading or review-status copy. Existing 2026 portraits remain unchanged.

Admin surfaces:

- Quiet, high-density operational layout.
- Tables, filters, job status badges, retry actions, and audit metadata are more important than visual drama.
- Admin uses its own `src/app/admin/layout.tsx` and never renders the public navigation, next-session rail, or public footer.
- Desktop uses a collapsible shadcn Sidebar. Mobile uses the Sidebar Sheet drawer. `Cmd+K` and `Ctrl+K` open the section command palette.
- Admin density dials are fixed at variance 3/10, motion 2/10, and density 9/10.
- The page header, metric strip, filters, dense table or mobile rows, and inline action feedback form the standard screen rhythm.
- Status is always communicated with a word and semantic color. Decorative status dots are not used.
- Technical metadata uses Geist Mono inside a bounded scroll area. Secrets, raw payload, and personal Telegram identifiers are never rendered.
- Destructive or publication-wide actions require AlertDialog. Reversible pause, hide, draft, and reject actions are preferred over deletion.
- Admin tables are real shadcn Table components at desktop and readable stacked rows below the table breakpoint.
- Forms use shadcn Field, Input, Select or native select with the same tokens, Textarea, Checkbox, and inline `useActionState` feedback.
- Loading uses layout-matched Skeleton blocks. Empty states explain the next safe action. Errors preserve the user’s data and offer a retry.
- The AI prompt registry is a dense operational list rather than a card grid. Editing opens a titled Dialog with the instruction, task template, allowed variables, protected response contract, and recent version history.
- Each AI prompt version includes its OpenRouter model and response-token limit. Model selection is searchable, shows safe context/pricing metadata, and never exposes provider credentials.
- Saving a prompt creates a draft. Publishing requires AlertDialog confirmation and clearly states that only future processing uses the new version.
- `/admin/systems` groups external APIs, RSS/social sources, and scheduled checks by service. It shows the last real check, next expected check, pause/error state, and a direct path to the relevant settings.
- `/admin/schedules` edits only timing and pause state for allowlisted jobs. Automatic retries are not implemented; their count is not editable. Cron expressions, shell commands, secrets, and arbitrary worker arguments are never editable.
- `/admin/issues` groups reader reports by state, keeps the original article snapshot and exposes only safe diagnostic context. The public report form sits next to the source at the end of a news article.
- `/admin/users` shows each user's RaceSide Plus status and access end date. Granting or extending Plus uses a confirmed dialog with a read-only user identity, duration, mandatory internal reason and a server-calculated end-date preview. The action copy is `Выдать Plus`, `Продлить Plus` or `Выдать снова`; the normal grant flow cannot shorten active access.
- Published news and social items use a confirmed `Убрать из ленты` action. It is visually secondary, reversible, and never presented as permanent deletion.
- `/admin/ai` compares AI API and X API spend in one date chart; their limits and accounting remain independent.
- The overview section is called `Что требует внимания`; it contains active failures, warnings, stale checks and unconfirmed states. Intentional pauses are neutral and appear separately. Aggregate counts cover the full matching history; the overview displays at most eight attention items.
- Mobile admin navigation closes after choosing a section. Route changes focus the main content; cancelling the drawer returns focus to its trigger.
- Paid access and Lava auto-renewal are separate. Revoking access does not silently cancel renewal; cancellation requires a reason and provider confirmation, and preserves paid periods.
- Manual payment confirmation displays the order currency, net amount, operation and explicit Moscow payment date. It never substitutes today's date.
- LIVE radio and background AI use the same UTC daily and rolling 30-day budget. Instruction editing is limited to the declared prompt catalog; radio is outside that editor.

## Motion

RaceSide LIVE uses a standalone viewport-height shell, a 52px HUD, persistent timing tower and selected driver on desktop, a central tabbed workspace, and an events/radio rail. Mobile replaces columns with workspace tabs. Only bounded panels scroll. It retains Geist, semantic theme tokens, team accents and the existing track assets. Continuous marker movement is isolated from the timing and feed React trees.

LIVE has a compact `LIVE` / «Перемотка» switch in its HUD. Only rewind opens a slim bottom row with pause, ±5-second buttons, a slider covering at most three minutes and the current offset. The row stays below the workspace and respects the mobile safe area. All panels share its presentation clock; transport health remains current. Desktop retains one HUD row; narrow screens separate the mode controls, session status and weather without horizontal overflow. Use the existing Button and Slider, Geist, tabular time values and restrained selected surfaces. The active LIVE button uses the brand accent. An expired pause explains that its moment left the window and offers «Продолжить с начала окна». Existing full-session Replay controls stay unchanged.

The LIVE 2D map divides its centerline into the circuit's marshal sectors without drawing persistent boundaries or labels. While OpenF1 reports one or more sector yellows, only those path segments receive a yellow core and restrained glow; a global clear removes every highlight. The map's accessible label names the active sectors.

Motion should imply speed and state change without becoming a racing-game HUD.

Use:

- 150-250ms hover, focus, selection, loading, and control feedback;
- transform and opacity for animation;
- subtle timing/ticker motion only where it represents real product state.

Avoid:

- orchestrated page-load sequences for task surfaces;
- bounce/elastic motion;
- scroll effects that hide content until JS runs;
- animating layout properties;
- heavy blur on scrolling containers.

Every motion pattern needs a `prefers-reduced-motion` alternative.

## Content Design

UI language is Russian by default.

Write like a product designer speaking to an F1 fan:

- "Сделать прогноз" instead of "Создание прогноза".
- "Любимые команды" instead of "Список избранных команд".
- "Нет свежих новостей" instead of "Данные отсутствуют".
- "Попробовать еще раз" instead of "Повторить запрос".

Avoid:

- field-name language;
- internal statuses as user copy;
- API terminology;
- formal bureaucratic wording;
- placeholders that repeat labels.

Admin copy can be more operational, but still human and direct.
